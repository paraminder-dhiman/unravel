UNRAVEL · Torn cloth and frayed threads for Blender
====================================================

QUICK START
1. Select a cloth mesh. With UVs, threads can also follow the weave.
2. Right-click in the viewport and choose Make Unravel
   (or use the Unravel tab in the N sidebar).
3. Click Paint Tears and paint red where the cloth should tear.
4. Click Done Painting. Holes, thick edges and threads appear.
5. Try a look from Presets, then fine-tune in the Unravel tab.


THE UNRAVEL TAB (press N in the viewport)
Scale
  Unravel expects real-world size (1 unit = 1 metre) and sets Scale from
  the scene's Unit Scale. If the cloth is 10 times too big, set Scale
  to 10. Raise it on a correctly sized cloth for chunkier threads.

Tears
  Hole Size ........ how far holes eat into the painted area (0 = no holes)
  Edge Noise ....... how ragged the edges are
  Noise Scale ...... size of the ragged bits
  Edge Smoothing ... smooths the stepped outline of each hole
                      (spikes and tiny loose bits left by cutting are
                      cleaned up for you, even on triangle meshes)
  Detail ........... extra subdivision for finer edges (heavier)
  Detect Holes ..... gives holes already in the mesh torn edges and
                      threads, with no painting. Their outlines stay
                      exactly as modelled
    Ignore Boundaries  leaves the mesh's own outer edges alone (hems,
                      cuffs, necklines). Untick it to fray those too
    Boundary Size .. open loops longer than this count as outer edges.
                      It is set for you the first time you turn on
                      Detect Holes; the line below shows what it found
    Fix one hole ... in Edit Mode, select an edge on its border and use
                      Mark as Tear or Clear Tear (also in the Edge menu)
    Refresh button . finds the holes again after you edit the mesh
  Thickness ........ how thick the torn edges look. It eases off on
                      tight curves so the edge can't fold through itself
  Lip Width ........ how far the thickness reaches from each hole
  Whole Cloth ...... makes the whole cloth solid

Threads (Fray Threads and Stray Threads)
  Two layers of threads: short dense fray, and long loose strays.
  The checkbox in each title turns that layer on or off. Each layer's
  settings are in groups you can open and close.
  Viewport Density . show fewer threads while working; renders show all
  Shape ............ how many threads, how long, and how wide
  Direction ........ which way the threads point
    Flow ........... 0 follows the weave of the cloth, 1 points the
                      threads into the hole
    Aim ............ Across Edge points each thread straight into the
                      hole from where it grows (works for any shape).
                      Toward Centre points threads at the middle of
                      their hole (tidiest on round holes)
    Direction Random  breaks up how evenly the threads point
    Follow Curvature  bends threads along the surface the cloth would
                      have had, instead of sticking straight out
    Stay in Hole ... shortens threads that would reach past the far
                      side of their hole
  Style ............ Clumping, Curl, Frizz and Droop: the character of
                      the threads
    Clumping ....... 1 gathers the tips together, -1 gathers the roots
                      into tufts that fan out like grass, 0 is off
  Bridges .......... threads that stay joined across narrow gaps, and
                      how much they sag
  Surface Contact .. keeps threads from sinking into the cloth

Thread Material
  Take Colour from Cloth: threads use the cloth's own material, coloured
  by where each thread grows. Untick it to pick your own colour.

Strands
  Hand-drawn loose threads. Use Add Strand (or Shift+A > Unravel Strand),
  draw on the cloth, then press Tab when done. The X on a strand's box 
  deletes it.


ANIMATION AND SIMULATION
  Before you simulate or rig the cloth, click Bake Tears (bottom of the
  Tears panel). This cuts the holes into the mesh, so the simulation sees
  them and the cloth flaps around its tears. Thickness, threads and
  strands stay live and follow the cloth. Unbake Tears brings the live
  tears back so you can paint and tweak them again; bake again afterwards.
  Draw strands while the cloth is in its resting shape (frame 1).


EXPORT (Apply Unravel, bottom of the main panel)
  Turns the cloth and threads into plain objects at the current frame.
  Threads as Mesh: thin tubes for FBX, glTF and game engines. Sides and
    Detail set the weight; the face count is shown after applying.
  Threads as Hair: hair curves for Blender, Alembic and USD.
  Rigged cloth: keep Skin Threads to Rig ticked so the threads bend with it.
  Restore Unravel brings the live setup back from a hidden backup.
  Animated cloth: no need to apply. Export the live scene to Alembic;
    the cloth and threads animate.


TIPS
  · Every Seed has a refresh button for a quick new variation.
  · Presets only change the look. Your paint is never touched.
  · Everything is undoable with Ctrl+Z.
  · All Unravel actions are also under Object > Unravel and in F3 search.


TROUBLESHOOTING
  No threads appear
    · Paint some red: threads only grow along torn edges.
    · Without UVs, set Flow above 0 (the panel warns you if there
      are no UVs).
    · Check Viewport Density is above 0.
  Threads look white
    · Switch the viewport to Rendered (Z > Rendered).
  Root Width and Tip Width seem to do nothing
    · Solid view always draws threads as thin lines. Switch to Material
      Preview or Rendered (Z key) to see their width.
    · Widths are real-world sizes and stop at 2 cm. For a cloth that is
      modelled too big, raise Scale instead.
  The viewport is slow
    · Lower Viewport Density, Detail, or Resolution.
